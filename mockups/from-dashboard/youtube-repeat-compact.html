Why the cover is the live video, not a thumbnail

YouTube's embed rules (developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality, read 2026-10-07): "Embedded players must have a viewport that is at least 200px by 200px", and nothing may be drawn over the player. A 320-wide 16:9 video is only 180 tall, so the cover is a 320 × 200 player (thin black bands top and bottom) with nothing on it. Swapping in a still image would mean hiding the player, and the rules say only "no changes not described by the API docs" about that. I'd rather not rely on that wording.

Two ways it could live on your desktop

  • In the tab: a "compact" button on /repeat shrinks the page to this card, draggable inside the browser window. You make the browser window small yourself. Works in every browser.
  • Popped out (Chrome/Edge desktop): the browser's Document Picture-in-Picture lets a page open an always-on-top mini-window, and the card would live there and float over other apps. Untested: whether the YouTube player keeps playing when it's moved into that window, or has to reload. That's the first thing I'd prove before building on it. In that window the OS frame does the dragging, so the grip strip becomes a plain title.
